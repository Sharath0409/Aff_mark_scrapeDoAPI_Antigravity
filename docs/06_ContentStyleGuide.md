# RemoteProstor Content Style Guide

## Purpose

This document defines the editorial standards for every article published on RemoteProstor.

Its purpose is to ensure that all published content maintains a consistent voice, high editorial quality, and provides genuine value to readers.

These standards apply to both Commercial and Informational articles.

---

# Editorial Philosophy

Every article published on RemoteProstor should exist to solve a real problem.

Readers should finish an article with greater confidence, better understanding, and practical knowledge they can immediately apply.

Content should educate before it attempts to influence purchasing decisions.

Helping the reader always takes priority over selling products.

---

# Target Audience

RemoteProstor primarily serves readers in the United States.

Content should therefore:

- Use American English.
- Use U.S. spelling conventions.
- Reference U.S. workplace practices where appropriate.
- Use U.S. units of measurement unless both systems improve clarity.
- Consider the expectations of American remote workers.

The writing should remain accessible to readers with varying technical backgrounds.

---

# Writing Tone

Every article should sound like it was written by an experienced professional with practical knowledge of the topic.

The writing should be:

- Professional
- Helpful
- Friendly
- Confident
- Honest
- Practical
- Objective

Avoid sounding robotic, overly academic, or excessively casual.

The goal is to build trust through expertise rather than personality.

---

# Writing Style

Articles should be easy to read.

Sentences should be concise without feeling abrupt.

Paragraphs should remain focused on a single idea.

Transitions between sections should feel natural.

Technical concepts should be explained clearly without unnecessary complexity.

Whenever possible, explain concepts using practical workplace examples.

---

# Reader-First Principle

Every paragraph should answer one of the following questions:

- What problem does the reader have?
- Why does this matter?
- How can the reader solve it?
- What should the reader do next?

If a paragraph answers none of these questions, it should not be included.

---

# Accuracy

All factual information should be accurate to the best available knowledge.

Where recommendations depend on established workplace guidance, reference recognized standards when appropriate.

Information should never be invented to make an article appear more authoritative.

If uncertainty exists, acknowledge it honestly.

---

# EEAT Principles

Every article should demonstrate:

## Experience

Content should reflect practical understanding rather than theoretical summaries.

Readers should feel that the advice comes from someone familiar with real-world remote work environments.

---

## Expertise

Technical concepts should be explained correctly.

Recommendations should reflect accepted best practices.

Specialized topics should be handled carefully without exaggeration.

---

## Authoritativeness

Articles should remain focused, comprehensive, and well organized.

Strong topical coverage helps establish authority over time.

Authority is earned through consistency and quality rather than promotional language.

---

## Trustworthiness

Readers should always know why a recommendation is being made.

Affiliate relationships should never influence factual accuracy.

Balanced discussions should include both strengths and limitations where appropriate.

---

# OSHA Alignment

Whenever an article discusses:

- Ergonomics
- Desk setup
- Monitor placement
- Sitting posture
- Standing desks
- Workplace safety
- Repetitive strain prevention

Recommendations should align with generally accepted OSHA ergonomic guidance whenever applicable.

The goal is to encourage healthier and safer work habits without presenting medical advice.

---

# Search Intent

Every article should satisfy a single primary search intent.

Before writing begins, identify whether the topic is:

- Informational
- Commercial

The article structure, headings, and recommendations should remain aligned with that intent throughout the entire article.

Content should never drift into unrelated topics.

---

# Article Structure

Every article should follow a logical progression.

Typical flow:

- Introduction
- Problem definition
- Explanation
- Practical guidance
- Supporting sections
- Frequently asked questions
- Conclusion

Commercial articles may additionally include product comparisons and recommendations.

---

# Heading Structure

Headings should guide the reader naturally through the article.

Each heading should introduce a meaningful topic.

Avoid generic headings that provide little value.

Headings should be descriptive enough that readers can quickly scan the page.

---

# Content Depth

Articles should answer the reader's question completely.

The objective is to eliminate the need for readers to search elsewhere for basic information.

Depth should come from usefulness rather than word count.

Long articles should remain engaging throughout.

---

# Examples

Whenever appropriate, include practical examples.

Examples should reflect realistic remote work situations.

Practical scenarios help readers understand recommendations more quickly than abstract explanations.

---

# Actionable Advice

Readers should leave every article knowing exactly what they can do next.

Whenever possible, provide:

- Practical steps
- Best practices
- Checklists
- Decision frameworks
- Common mistakes to avoid

Advice should be immediately useful.

---

# Internal Linking

Internal links should improve the reader's journey.

Links should:

- Be contextually relevant.
- Help readers explore related topics.
- Appear naturally.
- Never interrupt reading flow.

Links should support education rather than increase page views.

---

# Commercial Content Standards

Commercial articles should help readers make informed purchasing decisions.

Product discussions should remain balanced.

Explain:

- Who the product is suitable for.
- Where it performs well.
- Potential limitations.
- Important buying considerations.

Affiliate links should support genuine recommendations rather than aggressive sales.

---

# Informational Content Standards

Informational articles should prioritize education.

They should:

- Solve problems.
- Explain concepts clearly.
- Build topical authority.
- Naturally support related commercial articles where appropriate.

Educational value should always remain the primary objective.

---

# Filler Content

Filler content is prohibited.

Avoid:

- Repeating the same idea.
- Generic introductions.
- Empty motivational statements.
- Artificial keyword repetition.
- Unnecessary summaries.
- Obvious explanations.

Every sentence should contribute meaningful value.

---

# Keyword Usage

Keywords should appear naturally.

Do not force keywords into awkward sentences.

Semantic relevance and readability should take priority over keyword density.

Content should read naturally for humans.

---

# Readability

Articles should be easy to scan.

Use:

- Clear headings.
- Short paragraphs.
- Logical progression.
- Lists where appropriate.
- Simple language without oversimplification.

Readers should quickly locate the information they need.

---

# Visual Content

Images should support understanding rather than decoration.

Every image should have a clear educational purpose.

Images should complement the written content instead of replacing it.

---

# Conclusion

The conclusion should summarize the key takeaways without repeating the entire article.

Readers should finish with a clear understanding of:

- The main recommendation.
- The practical next step.
- The overall takeaway.

No new topics should be introduced in the conclusion.

---

# Guiding Principle

Every article should leave the reader thinking:

> "This answered exactly what I wanted to know, and I trust the advice enough to come back again."

If an article does not achieve this, it is not ready for publication.

---

This Content Style Guide is governed by the current Architecture Freeze.

Minor wording improvements and factual corrections are permitted provided they do not alter the intent of this guide.
